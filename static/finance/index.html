The Financial Statement Workbook

Type the raw figures from any set of statements into the columns below. Every subtotal, ratio, growth rate and chart recalculates as you go — with the arithmetic shown, so you can check the work rather than trust it.

Classified Balance Sheet

A point-in-time statement: assets on the left of the equation, the claims against them on the right. Three dates are shown because turnover ratios need an opening and closing balance — so computing them for the prior year takes a balance sheet date that only the previous year’s filing carries.

Enter figures to check the accounting equation.

Enter negatives with a leading minus or in parentheses — an accumulated deficit of 14,264 is (14,264) or -14264.

Multiple-Step Income Statement

Revenues and expenses over a period, stepped down through gross margin and operating income (EBIT) to net income.

Interest expense and market price are not always broken out on the face of the statement. Fill them where you have them — they unlock times interest earned, return on assets, P/E and dividend yield.

Statement of Shareholders’ Equity

A rollforward, not a snapshot: each equity account moves from its beginning balance to its ending balance. Net income adds, dividends and buybacks subtract.

Enter figures to check the equity rollforward.

Statement of Cash Flows

Operating, investing and financing activities reconciling beginning cash to ending cash. Enter outflows as negatives.

Enter figures to check the cash reconciliation.

Ratio Worksheet

Every measure below shows its numerator and denominator with your actual figures substituted in, laid out the way the worksheet does. The prior-year column fills in wherever the data supports it.

Trends & Compound Annual Growth

Six years of any metric. Year-over-year growth appears under each row; the compound annual growth rate is computed across the full span.

CAGR = (Ending ÷ Beginning)1/n − 1, where n is the number of intervals between the first and last year entered — five intervals across six years, not six.
